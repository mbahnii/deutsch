# "Ein bisschen" and "einen" + verb

Why these expressions have an article although there seems to be no noun.

## ein bisschen

It is **not an adverb**. *Bisschen* (das Bisschen) is really a noun, the diminutive of *der Biss* ("bite"), literally "a little piece". So *ein* here is the article of that noun. It does not decline:

- *ein bisschen Zucker*
- *mit einem bisschen Zucker*

Similar in meaning: *ein wenig*.

## einen + verb

A colloquial construction **einen + verb** with the noun left out. *Einen* is masculine accusative and something like *einen Schluck* or *einen Zug* is meant. The article stays because the word is, as it were, omitted:

- *einen trinken* — have a drink
- *einen rauchen* — have a cigarette
- *einen heben* — "knock one back" (colloquial)

You do not need this for the DTZ, but such expressions occur in conversation. Some similar expressions with *einen* are vulgar, so avoid them in polite speech. If you meet an unfamiliar one, ask in which context it was said.
